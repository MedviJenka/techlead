# Objection Handling

Classify the objection, judge its severity, and pick a response strategy. Never invent concessions.

| Objection  | Signals                              | Severity cues                                         | Strategy                                                           |
|------------|--------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Price      | "too expensive", comparing quotes    | "can't afford" (high) vs "is there flexibility" (low) | Clarify value and scope; pricing questions go to a human           |
| Timing     | "not now", "next quarter"            | Firm date (low) vs vague (medium)                     | Agree a concrete time to reconnect; recommend `wait` with `due_at` |
| Authority  | "need to ask my partner/boss"        | Usually low                                           | Offer material or a joint call with the decision maker             |
| Trust      | "is this legit", asks for references | Medium                                                | Offer references or case studies the business actually has         |
| Competitor | "we use X", "got a better offer"     | Locked-in contract (high)                             | Find the gap X does not cover; never disparage                     |
| Need       | "not sure we need this"              | High if repeated                                      | Re-qualify; consider `nurture`                                     |

- Severity guides scoring: a high-severity unresolved objection lowers intent and fit.
- Pricing, discounts and contract terms are always human decisions.
