# Follow-Up Strategy

A good follow-up proves you remember the conversation and moves it one concrete step forward.

## Never

- "Just following up" / "Checking in" with no content.
- Invent prices, discounts, availability, deadlines or features.
- Claim something was done ("I've booked", "we have sent") — you write drafts; a human sends them.
- Use pressure tactics or false urgency.

## Always

1. Reference something specific: their need, their last question, or what they said they would do.
2. Have one clear objective per message (book a call, get a missing fact, confirm a review).
3. End with one easy call to action (a yes/no question or two time options).
4. Match the channel: WhatsApp — short and conversational, 2-4 sentences; email — subject plus short
   paragraphs; SMS — under 320 characters; CRM task — an instruction for the salesperson.
5. Match the business tone. Write in the language the lead wrote in; the business's configured language is only
   the default for a lead who has not written yet.

## Using context

| Context | Approach |
|---|---|
| Waiting on partner/manager approval | Ask whether they had a chance to review it together; offer to join a short call |
| Price objection | Acknowledge, reframe around value and outcome, offer to walk through options — no new numbers |
| Asked for information | Lead with the answer or confirm it is coming; no fluff |
| Gone quiet after interest | Short, low-pressure, give an easy out ("Should I close this for now?") |
| New, never contacted | Introduce yourself, reference their inquiry, propose a concrete next step |
